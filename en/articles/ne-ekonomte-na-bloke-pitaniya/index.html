Why you shouldn’t skimp on the power supply

A story from an order: the customer kept their old power supply on a friend’s advice — and at home the PC wouldn’t start. How we found the cause and how it ended.

The customer and I spent about three months choosing the configuration and went through a huge number of options. We settled on a Ryzen 5 8400F, a 12 GB RTX 3080 EVGA FTW3 Ultra, 32 GB of DDR5 at 6000 MHz, a Maxsun B650M Challenger motherboard and a Powercase Byteflow case.

The old power supply

The customer wanted to use their own power supply — an old 1275 W Thermaltake Toughpower XT Platinum. I said right away that in that case I couldn’t give a warranty on the computer: an old PSU can act up. We ordered a new 850 W 1stPlayer NGDP Gold. It arrived, but a couple of days later the customer asked to return it — a “tech-savvy friend” had convinced them the old one still had plenty of life in it. I couldn’t change their mind.

We built the PC and tested it under load at my place — everything worked. The PSU didn’t have enough 8-pin cables for the graphics card, so I had to add my own cable. I also cleaned the PSU itself and lubricated its fan.

What happened

At the customer’s home, the computer showed no picture. The next day I came over with all my diagnostic gear, and four hours later we figured it out: the PSU was to blame. It may have degraded after the stress tests, where the computer drew about 600 W, and something happened to the 12 V rail. With my test graphics card, which needs no extra power, everything worked, but with any card that needs extra power there was a problem.

Through trial and error and lots of BIOS resets, we found out that the PSU periodically sent a voltage spike to the graphics card power connectors, the motherboard showed POST code 99 at boot, and the computer went into protection mode.

Luckily, the PSU didn’t take anything down with it. We ordered the new 850 W 1stPlayer NGDP Gold again — now 2,000 ₽ more expensive because of the rush. The customer installed it themselves, and everything worked.

Buy cheap, buy twice. The power supply is not the place to save money or to keep an old one and hope for the best.

How to check that the PSU is to blame

The simplest way is to connect a power supply you know works. Another time a computer wouldn’t turn on, and we suspected the PSU: we connected the one from the computer next to it — and everything worked. The old Gigabyte unit had served for about 10 years and, fortunately, didn’t take anything with it. We installed a new 500 W Cougar STC — even with some headroom for a build with an i5-6400 and a GTX 1050 Ti.

Why I build alone

On that same Ryzen 5 8400F build, the customer wanted to assemble it together with me. While I was cleaning the old PSU, they started sorting the ARGB controller cables — and hadn’t wiped their hands well after eating. At the first start-up there was a sharp burning smell, and the lighting blinked and went out. The controller had to be replaced. Since then, I build computers only on my own — without customers or friends around.
