Why Palit graphics cards run hot and how to fix it

A Hot Spot near 105 °C, fluctuating power draw and noise are a typical problem with Palit and similar brands. The cause is the cooler screws, and it can be fixed.

An RTX 3070 Palit GamingPro came to me for servicing. The GPU temperature stayed around 70 °C — that’s normal — but the card heated up even under minimal load, the Hot Spot shot up to 104–105 °C, the power draw fluctuated a lot and the fans were very loud.

What causes it

This is a typical problem with Palit and brands whose coolers are built the same way. The stock screws that hold the cooler to the board have partial threads. Because of this, the whole cooler sags and doesn’t press firmly against the chip — you can see it in the thermal paste imprint. On top of that, this card had dead thermal pads.

What I did

  • Replaced the stock screws with fully threaded M2.5 × 6 screws — the mounting pressure became normal.
  • Replaced the thermal paste with ID-Cooling Frost X25.
  • Replaced the thermal pads with Laird Tputty 607 liquid pads.

The result

The GPU temperature stayed about the same, but the Hot Spot dropped to 82–83 °C. The power draw became stable, and the fans got noticeably quieter.

If you have a Palit or a similar card with a normal GPU temperature but a Hot Spot above 100 °C, the mounting pressure is most likely to blame. Replacing the screws and thermal interfaces fixes it.
