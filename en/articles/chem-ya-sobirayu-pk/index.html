What I use to build and service PCs

The consumables, cleaning fluids, tools and diagnostic gear I use when building and servicing PCs — and why I chose them.

Here’s what I use when building computers, and why. Think of it as a guide: these things make the work easier, improve the quality of the builds and give more ways to diagnose problems. The photo shows far from everything I use.

Consumables

  • Phase-change thermal paste — Honeywell PTM7950 SP. It transfers heat superbly — only liquid metal does better. Unlike regular paste, it doesn’t get pumped out, fills micro-scratches and micro-cracks on the surfaces well and lasts a very long time.
  • Thermal pads — Fehonda. Nothing supernatural: simply the best top-tier pads for the price.
  • Liquid thermal pads — Laird Tputty 607. The best liquid pads for the price: they work well at thicknesses up to 1 mm and on components that don’t run too hot.

Cleaning and lubricating fluids

  • Kalosha petroleum solvent — to remove old thermal paste and pad residue from boards and to degrease surfaces. It costs next to nothing and handles all of this perfectly.
  • Flux-Off — for sockets and hard-to-reach spots. Not dirt cheap, but not expensive either; the main thing is that it comes in a pressurized can and dissolves thermal paste quite well.
  • PMS-200 silicone oil — for fan bearings. The best for the price, and its consistency is almost ideal for these bearings.

The basics

  • Cotton pads and swabs — any kind, the cheapest will do.
  • Microfiber cloths — just right for wiping off dust.
  • Cable ties — all kinds, but short thin ones and long thick ones are the handiest: they make cable management easier.
  • Masking tape — I write the customer’s name on it and use it to keep the component boxes together with their warranty cards. For packing — regular tape.
  • Bubble wrap — a packing essential.
  • CR2032 batteries — I bought a pack of 24 Duracell: the price was nice, and they measure fine.
  • Screws — I’ve collected a lot of them over the years. If you buy a new case for your build, everything you need comes in the box.

Tools

  • Screwdrivers — three sets right now: an old 99-ruble set from Fix Price, an inexpensive AliExpress set with lots of small bits and a Kingsdun electric screwdriver. Before it I had a Xiaomi one — it died after a year. The Kingsdun is cheaper and almost twice as powerful: 0.35 N·m versus 0.2, though sometimes even that isn’t enough.
  • Tweezers — to fish out dropped screws and peel off thermal pads when you don’t want to get your hands dirty.
  • Pliers from AliExpress — indispensable for cable management: they snip cable ties in one go.
  • A scalpel — for opening boxes.
  • Two USB sticks: one with various Windows images, the other with BIOS firmware.

Time savers

  • A SATA SSD with a SATA-to-USB adapter: it holds testing software and installers for the basic programs.
  • Separate power and reset buttons for the motherboard’s front-panel header — so I don’t have to short the pins with a screwdriver when testing.
  • An adapter for unscrewing the motherboard standoffs in the case.
  • M2.5 × 6 screws for Palit and similar graphics cards — they fix weak cooler mounting pressure caused by stock screws with partial threads.

For diagnostics

  • A speaker — the most basic tool of a PC builder. Not always informative or convenient, but as cheap and accessible as it gets.
  • A POST card — if you get one, make sure it supports PCIe. It shows error codes at startup, if there are any.
  • A power supply tester — shows whether the PSU works and the voltage on each rail.
  • A pyrometer — to measure temperatures from the outside, for example on the VRM.
  • A multimeter — a digital auto-ranging one: no need to rack your brain over which measuring mode to pick.
